## 1. Project Summary

### Project Name

```
AYYUR — The Machines We Left Behind
```

### Summary (≈170 words, max 1500 characters)

```
AYYUR is an interactive web museum that tells the stories of the machines humanity left on the Moon, on Mars and in deep space: Apollo lunar modules, Surveyor, Viking, Spirit and Opportunity, InSight, Ingenuity, Pioneer 10 and more.

Visitors spin NASA globes to find where each machine rests, follow its mission on a timeline that flies the globe to each event, inspect NASA 3D models, and open NASA's Eyes on the Solar System without leaving the site. For young explorers, the same facts become games, a quiz, collectible cards and printable colouring books.

The challenge asks for storytelling about discarded equipment. AYYUR answers it by turning "space junk" into memory: every machine has a name, a story, the reason it went quiet, and what it still teaches us. Every fact, date and photo comes from NASA (NSSDCA and NASA Science), and every source is listed on the site.

It matters because these objects are humanity's first heritage beyond Earth. AYYUR makes that heritage free, accurate and accessible to everyone, in English, French, Spanish and Arabic.
```

## 3. Project Information

### Project Details *(paste into the rich-text box; use the bold headings)*

```
What it does
AYYUR is a web museum of 13 groups of NASA machines left on the Moon, on Mars and in deep space. For each machine, visitors can:
• find where it rests on a rotating NASA globe of the Moon or Mars;
• follow its mission on a timeline: each event flies the globe to where it happened;
• read its story, key dates, and why it went quiet;
• inspect its NASA 3D model, or open NASA's Eyes on the Solar System inside the page.

For young explorers (6–12)
• Four games (mystery machine, rebuild the mission, photo puzzle, memory) and a quiz.
• Collectible "Memory" cards with the real NASA photo and facts.
• Printable and on-screen colouring books (Pioneer 10, Apollo 11 "Eagle").

How it works
A static web app: React 19, TypeScript and Vite; 3D with three.js and React Three Fiber; NASA glTF models compressed with glTF-Transform; NASA photos resized to WebP with sharp. All facts live in one data file, each linked to its NASA source. Deployed on Vercel: https://ayyuur.vercel.app — code: https://github.com/Haytame37/space-apps-2026.

Benefits and impact
• Turns "discarded equipment" into shared heritage that people can visit and remember.
• Accurate: every fact, date and photo is traced to NASA (NSSDCA, NASA Science); a Sources page lists them all.
• Accessible: English, French, Spanish and Arabic (right to left), keyboard use, phone layout, reduced-motion support. Free, no account, no tracking.
• Useful in classrooms: short stories, games and printable books.

What makes it creative
Each machine is told as a character with a life: launch, landing, work, last contact, and what remains. The timeline links time to place, and the same NASA facts become a museum for adults and a playground for children.

Business model
Learning is free: the museum, games, quiz and book previews need no account and show no ads. Revenue comes from keepsakes, at prices benchmarked on similar products: printable colouring book €2.90, printed book €6.90, class pack of 10 books €49, collectible cards from €0.90, booster of 5 cards €3.90. Schools and families in Morocco come first, then events and partners.

What we considered
Factual accuracy (no unsourced claim), respect for NASA branding rules, child-friendly language, performance on low-end phones, accessibility, and clear disclosure of AI use.

Team
AYYUR: five engineering students from ENSA Béni Mellal, Morocco.
```

**If the form asks how SI was used, paste:**

```
• Code: an AI assistant (Claude, by Anthropic) helped write and review the website code. The team designed the project, checked and tested all code.
• Text: Claude helped draft and translate the texts (EN/FR/ES/AR) from the NASA sources listed. The team reviewed every fact against NSSDCA and NASA Science.
• Images: the line drawings of the two colouring books (Pioneer 10, Apollo 11 "Eagle") were generated with Google Gemini, then selected, checked and laid out by the team in Canva. They are marked as AI-generated in the books and on the site.
• Not AI-generated: all photos and 3D models (NASA), the 3D globes (NASA imagery), and the data.
```
